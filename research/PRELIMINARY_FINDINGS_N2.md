# Preliminary survey observations (n = 2)

**Snapshot date:** 24 October 2026  
**Status:** Early, non-representative sample. These observations are descriptive only and must not be presented as market-wide statistics.

## What the two responses said

| Theme | Response 1 | Response 2 | Cautious interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Respondent | Student | Social media content creator | Two different user contexts are represented, but the sample is too small for generalisation. |
| Content | Social posts, ads, website/product descriptions, college/organisation announcements | Social posts/captions | Social content is relevant to both; the first respondent also reviews several other formats. |
| Current checking | Self-check, written guidelines, AI tools, grammar tools | Self-check | The workflows vary. One response reports no fixed process. |
| Error frequency | Sometimes | Sometimes | Both selected “Sometimes”; no broader prevalence claim is justified. |
| Problems selected | Incorrect/outdated information, incorrect names, readability/accessibility, time spent checking | Inconsistent tone, incorrect colors/logos/images | The checker should address factual and terminology issues, but visual consistency also deserves validation with more users. |
| Feedback process | General feedback; respondent must work out the correction | No fixed process | Specific, actionable feedback may be useful; validate this in interviews. |
| Time | 16–30 minutes | Depends on content | Do not calculate average time or claim time saved from these two answers. |
| AI concerns | Unsupported evidence, misunderstanding rules, meaning changes, privacy | Misunderstanding rules, meaning changes | Preserving meaning and clear evidence are strong early product priorities. |
| Usefulness score | 3/5 | 4/5 | Both see some potential, but this is not enough to estimate product-market fit. |
| Features selected | Meaning-preserving corrections, accept/edit/reject, comments/approval | Approved-fact checks, meaning-preserving corrections, accept/edit/reject | Both chose controlled corrections; one also selected evidence checking and one selected team review. |
| Follow-up interview | No | No | These two respondents did not volunteer for follow-up; recruit interviewees separately. |

## Product decisions this supports so far

1. Keep corrections human-controlled: accept, edit or dismiss; do not silently rewrite drafts.
2. Show the exact source and explanation for factual/brand warnings.
3. Make review feedback specific and connect comments to a draft/version.
4. Continue validating visual asset checks with creators who actually publish image-based content.
5. Keep the interface compact. One respondent specifically said the survey had too many options; simplify survey options where possible without removing necessary answer categories.

## What remains unknown

- Which issue types are most costly across a broader population.
- Whether the target users would pay for or regularly use BrandCheck.
- Whether visual checks, source expiry, readability or workflow approval are the highest priority across users.
- Whether BrandCheck actually reduces review time or false warnings.
- How well findings and suggestions perform on a labelled test set.

## Next steps

Collect more relevant survey responses, recruit 3–5 interviews, review public guidelines from 3–5 organisations, and run the labelled functional test plan. Report updated denominators and limitations.
